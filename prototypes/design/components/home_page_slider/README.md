# Tennis AI Coach — Blurred HTML5 tennis video demo

Open `home_page_slider.html` for the centered, video-only preview. All CSS and JavaScript files live directly in `home_page_slider/`; reference PNGs remain in `assets/`, while the video lives in `../../../components/sliders/home-page-video-slider/assets/`. The full hero is integrated into `../../pages/index.html` and shares `home-hero.css`, `video-demo.js`, and `hero-alignment.js`.

The HTML includes a **real HTML5 `<video>`**, blurred video playing at 0.3× speed, and video-synchronized coaching messages. Each loop plays the first 4.5 seconds of source footage at 0.3× speed, with a 5.5-second freeze at source timestamp 2.900s and a second 3.5-second freeze at 3.700s. The first pause starts after about 9.7 seconds of viewing; the second follows 2.7 seconds of playback after the first resumes. The resulting loop takes about 24 seconds plus seek latency. Shorter clips loop at their natural end and skip freeze timestamps beyond their duration; the source file is unchanged. The fluid page wrapper grows to 1360px, with the desktop video enlarged by a further 12% (about 989px wide and 665px tall). It extends left beneath the text section with a broader fade; the text stays above the video. An upper-right microphone has a visible 1.6-second glow pulse and expanding ring during playback and coaching pauses. At 1100px and below, the hero stacks into one column; the microphone and small message bubble remain inside the responsive video area. No external player or tracking library. The preview contains only the video slider; the full Home page retains the hero text and navigation.

Video loads from the bundled `../../../components/sliders/home-page-video-slider/assets/2018_Davis_Cup_Americas_Zone_-_Uruguay_vs_Mexico_-_04.webm`, with the existing Wikimedia URL as a fallback.

**Freeze-frame lines:** Manually positioned green strokes follow the supplied 1280×720 reference images: six strokes at 2.900s (arms and legs), then eight at 3.700s (arms, torso and legs). These are illustrative annotations, not detected joints or live tracking; no MediaPipe/model is loaded. After each exact-frame seek settles, the strokes draw one after another (300ms per stroke, starting every 350ms). All strokes are visible after about 2.05s at the first pause and 2.75s at the second. They remain until that pause's total duration (5.5s / 3.5s) ends, then disappear before playback resumes at 0.3×. Each pause happens once per loop, with its own coordinates; timers suspend when the banner/tab is hidden. Reduced-motion playback does not auto-start. `video-demo.js` owns the coordinates and pause timing; the transparent canvas maps source pixels through the existing centered cover crop and scale(1.07), including responsive resize and pixel density. Replacing the video requires recalibrating these manual coordinates. The cues are scripted visual feedback for this prototype, not inferred measurements or generated advice. No microphone permission, speech synthesis or audio playback is added; the video stays muted.




**Coaching interaction:** Camera, Analysis, and Audio connection badges remain at the top. The microphone retains its active pulse. A permanent Motion Tracking preview panel sits near the right edge, with READY/ANALYZING phase driven by the existing pause state. The fixed 52px coaching bubble shows gently pulsing waiting dots during playback/loading/seeks. During the 2.9s hold it types “Good arm position”, “Bend your knees”, and “Toss the ball higher”; during the 3.7s hold it types “Keep the racket position stable” and “Follow through”. The existing hold clock controls cue selection and typing (55ms per character), with no additional timers. Playback resumes into the waiting state. Hidden/offscreen time does not advance the hold; reduced motion disables waiting-dot animation. The first two strokes in each hold display small illustrative angle/vector labels, anchored through the same cover-crop/resize transform and revealed with the corresponding stroke. Labels clear with the canvas on resume. The second hold's final stroke runs from (587, 458) to (589, 482), a subtle 4.8° reduction from its previous vertical orientation with the same start and timing. The penultimate stroke runs from (561, 457) to (566, 482), slightly reducing its lean while retaining its diagonal direction and original timing. These values and coaching cues are scripted preview data, not measured observations or actual audio functionality.

**Hero alignment:** Description and CTA row share a responsive width measured from the rendered “Win more.” headline, with buttons filling that row (stacking on small screens). The three equal-width feature cards follow the measured “Train smarter.” boundary with compact gaps and responsive padding. Measurements refresh after font loading and resizing, preserving alignment across system fonts. The coaching bubble remains mounted and visible at a stable size and position, including loading, seeking, and errors; only its inner text span is updated or replaced visually by waiting dots.

**Lower-body preview data:** The last four strokes of each hold each reveal one illustrative knee-flexion, hip-height, or foot-vector label. They use the same stroke progress and source-coordinate mapping as the upper-body labels and clear with the canvas on resume. Small rotations follow the strokes while keeping text readable; compact screens stagger the leg labels to the left, away from the Motion Tracking panel. Values are authored demo data, not inferred biomechanics.

**Coaching timing:** The HTML preview uses its own elapsed-time clock, independent of video holds and seeks. Five sentences repeat in equal 4.8-second slots across the configured 24-second cycle: 3.3 seconds for typing/display and 1.5 seconds showing `...`. The microphone indicator stays active during both playback and analysis. Hidden/offscreen previews suspend the coaching clock.
